### Agile Software Project Managmenet

- "Agile" is a consultant buzzword

The story told by Agile consultants goes:

"In the beginning, three was Waterfall..."

Waterfall is the traditional engineering methodology:

- Phase 1: Collect Requirements
- Phase 2: Determine specifications
- Phase 3: Build the thing
- Phase 4: Give the user the thing, completed

"Agile" is, broadly, the idea that instead of having one sequence
and go through the phases in order, you need to have some sort of
iterative feedback loop.

## Agile Manifesto

Software projects should value:

- Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.
- Working software over comprehensive documentation.
- Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
- Responding to change over following a plan.

This is great for small to medium teams of developers, but managers
and executives *hate* it.

## Kanban

Toyota was trying to build cars.

They noticed that the thing they needed to optimize wasn't one car, it was the
*process* of building cars. So they build a system for:

- tracking the process
- tracking potential process improvements

Key thing stolen for software dev: Kanban Boards

Instead of cars, or car issues, we track software issues / stories.

## Scrum

A concrete set of agile processes (yay!)

Software dev is divided in to one or two week "sprints".

- The team starts with a working program.
- Each team member has tasks to work on.
- Everyone completes their tasks.
- That work is merged together into a new working program.
- There are meetings and stuff.
- There are admin team members with roles like talking to
the customer and assigning tasks.

This works well for effective teams and can easily degrade into
disjunction for dysfunctional teams.
