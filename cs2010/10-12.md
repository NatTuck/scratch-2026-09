## Boolean Logic

- Computer store data as bits.
- A bit distinguishes two values: 0/1, low/high, true/false, red/blue, etc.

Especially interesting: true/false

- Reasoning about true/false isn't new. Philosphers have been doing it for
millenia.
- George Boole formalized true/false reasoning mathematically in his
1847 book.
  - No computers, but we had telegraphs widely deployed.
  - Very early electric lightbulbs, but not widely deployed.
  - The first electric motors had been developed.

Numerical (integer) arithmetic:

- We work with integers as our values
- Basic operations: +, -, *, /, remainder
- That gives us some laws:
  - One integer is an additive identity A: x + A = x (0)
  - One integer is a multiplicative identity M: x * M = x (1)

Boolean logic (arithmetic) basics:

- Boolean values: true, false
- Three basic operations: AND, OR, NOT
- At least three common notations.

Derived operatoins:

- NAND (ab)', NOR (a+b)', XOR (ab')+(a'b)
